Running shoe shopping gets messy fast.
BriefBuy keeps the decision clear.

BriefBuy is an AI running shoe comparison app. You paste product links, and it turns the research into fit scores, ranked options, and shoes you can rule out. I built it with a data engineer and a frontend developer in four weeks.

01 Background

I had four weeks to build an AI product.
I shipped the design and the frontend.

In April 2026, a friend invited me to a Korean IT community challenge. Three people, four weeks, one working product. I was the only designer on the team.

We chose running shoes because the buying decision is genuinely hard. Foot shape, distance, budget, injury history. Every detail changes the right answer. Most people spend hours researching and still feel uncertain at checkout.

I designed the product and vibe-coded the entire frontend. It was the first time I shipped code I actually wrote. That changed how I think about what a designer can own.

02 The Problem

Chat is great at explaining.
It is bad at helping you decide.

By message ten, you can easily lose track. The longer a shoe conversation gets, the more your original conditions drift. AI has forgotten your wide feet and your budget.

A runner training for a first half marathon

Wide feet. Under $200. Nervous about blisters on long runs.

"I have wide feet and I am training for a half marathon. My budget is about $180. Is Hoka Clifton 10 or New Balance 1080 better for me?"
Both are strong options. The Clifton is lighter and soft. The 1080 has wide sizes and plush cushioning. For your feet and budget, I would start with the 1080.
"Can you keep the wide fit and blister issue in mind?"

Context drifts as the chat gets longer

Text is not a comparison surface

The research disappears the next day

The design challenge

Build a place where your profile, scores, and options stay visible while you chat.

04 Design

Chat on one side.
Decisions on the other.

The main design choice was a two panel layout. The chat is where users ask questions. The panel is where the decision stays organized.

The panel updates with every message, but it never moves. Users can always see their profile, candidates, fit scores, and rejected options.

Two panel layout

Left: Chat

Ask, compare, refine

Users paste links, ask follow ups, and narrow the list by fit, cushion, or price.

Right: Fixed Decision Panel

A workspace that stays visible

The profile, scores, rankings, and rejected shoes stay in one place while the chat changes.

Inside the design

Three components. Each solving a different part of the decision.

06 Outcome

Four weeks later,
we had a working product.

We tested five common cases for speed and accuracy. The main flow worked, and the recommendation matched the runner conditions we gave it.

The best result was caching. A repeated product link loaded in 63 milliseconds instead of about 13 seconds. That made repeat research feel instant.

07 What I Learned

Building fast taught me
where the product was strongest.

The biggest lesson was simple: memory is part of the design. BriefBuy worked because the decision panel stayed visible while the chat kept changing.

I also learned where I would push the product next. I still think users should be able to describe what they need first, without finding product links before they start.
